EVIDENCE, NOT GUESSWORK

What this map can tell you.

Sleeping in your car can count as camping. A road, parking lot, or public-land boundary by itself does not establish overnight permission.

How we label stops

Researched area: reviewed sources describe vehicle camping in this area; the marker is not an exact sleeping position. Car rest permitted: official time-limited rest policy and a recent opening-status check. Unknown: car access, precise camping position or current conditions are unresolved. Needs verification: community-mapped parking or a road whose camping permission has not been established. These lots appear only in research mode. Permission recorded: you recorded permission for your arrival date; stored only on this device.

Forest roads

Click a road or “Check a roadside spot” for the applicable forest rule, land status, and source maps. Administrative boundaries include private inholdings. In the Eastern Sierra, green zones intersect public Inyo forest ownership with county coverage and subtract mapped camping exclusions, wilderness and designated-only areas. Red polygons show the local prohibition. Other forests still have incomplete local geometry.

Parking lots, including Walmart

There is no chain-wide car-sleeping permission. Walmart’s published policy addresses RVs and requires individual store-manager permission, subject to local law. A 24-hour store or overnight vehicle-parking tag is not enough. Walmart policy

Campground availability

Recreation.gov openings are checked for your whole stay at the same campsite, cached for five minutes, and never served as current when the source fails. Walk-up means no vacancy is established. State and private booking inventory is not integrated.

Weak signal

The app, catalog, viewed details, and saved stops are stored on this device. Offline data shows its saved timestamp. Basemap tiles and unvisited data require a connection; this is not a downloaded navigation map.
